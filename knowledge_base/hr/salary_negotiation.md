# Salary Negotiation Guide

## Principles

1. **Never be the first to name a number** if possible — ask the interviewer for the budgeted range.
2. **Negotiate on total compensation**, not just base salary (consider health insurance, bonuses, equity, learning allowance, remote flexibility).
3. **Always negotiate** — companies expect it; a polite counter-offer rarely hurts you.
4. **Have a walk-away number** in mind before negotiations begin.

## Salary Research Resources

- LinkedIn Salary Insights
- Glassdoor / AmbitionBox (India-specific)
- Levels.fyi (for tech companies)
- NASSCOM / industry salary reports
- Peers and seniors in your network

## Sample Negotiation Script

> "Thank you for the offer — I'm very excited about this opportunity. Based on my research on market rates for this role and my specific skill set in [X, Y, Z], I was expecting something closer to [₹X]. Is there any flexibility in the base salary?"

If they say no:
> "I understand. Could we revisit this after 6 months once I've demonstrated my contributions?"

Or ask for non-salary benefits:
> "If the base is fixed, would it be possible to include a signing bonus / additional learning budget / remote work days?"

## For Freshers

- Do not quote a number without research — it can anchor negotiations too low.
- It is acceptable to ask: "What is the standard CTC for this role at your company for freshers?"
- Focus on the learning opportunity if the compensation is market-rate.
- Stipends during internship are usually non-negotiable; return offer post-internship is negotiable.

## Common Mistakes

- Accepting the first offer immediately without any question
- Naming a number dramatically above market rate without justification
- Revealing competing offers as leverage without being willing to use them
- Negotiating via email when a phone/video call is more effective
- Forgetting to get the final offer in writing
