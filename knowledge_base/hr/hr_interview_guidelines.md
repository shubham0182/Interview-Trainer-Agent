# HR Interview Guidelines

## Purpose of the HR Round

The HR interview assesses cultural fit, communication skills, motivation, and non-technical soft skills.
It typically follows technical rounds and evaluates whether a candidate aligns with the company's values.

## Most Common HR Questions

### Motivation and Fit
- Why do you want to work here?
- What do you know about our company?
- What attracted you to this role?
- Why are you leaving your current job? (or: Why are you looking for a new opportunity?)

### Career Goals
- Where do you see yourself in 3–5 years?
- What are your short-term and long-term career goals?
- What kind of manager brings out your best work?

### Compensation and Logistics
- What are your salary expectations?
- Are you open to relocation?
- What is your notice period?
- Do you have any competing offers?

### Values and Culture
- What does work-life balance mean to you?
- Describe your ideal company culture.
- What motivates you to do your best work?
- How do you handle constructive criticism?

## How to Answer Salary Questions

- Research market rates before the interview using platforms like LinkedIn Salary, Glassdoor, or AmbitionBox.
- Give a range, not a fixed number: "Based on my research and experience, I'm targeting ₹X–₹Y."
- Always express flexibility and openness to the full compensation package (benefits, learning budget, equity).
- For freshers: "I'm open to the company's standard package for this role, and I'm more focused on the learning opportunity."

## Questions to Ask the Interviewer

Asking thoughtful questions demonstrates genuine interest:
- "What does success look like in this role after 90 days?"
- "What are the biggest challenges someone in this role typically faces?"
- "How would you describe the team culture?"
- "What opportunities are there for learning and growth?"
- "What does the onboarding process look like?"

Avoid asking about salary/leave/benefits in early rounds unless the interviewer raises it.

## Red Flags HR Interviewers Watch For

- Speaking negatively about previous managers or companies
- Unclear motivation for applying (shows lack of research)
- Inconsistency between resume claims and answers
- Inability to give a concrete example when asked a behavioral question
- Overconfidence or underconfidence in self-assessment
