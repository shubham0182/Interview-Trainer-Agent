# Common Behavioral Interview Questions and Patterns

## Self-Introduction and Background

- Tell me about yourself. (Keep to 2 minutes: education → projects → why this role)
- Walk me through your resume.
- What are your greatest strengths? (Pick 2–3 that are relevant and back each with evidence)
- What is your greatest weakness? (Pick a real one; show what you've done to improve it)
- Why do you want this role / join this company?
- Where do you see yourself in 5 years?

## Work Style and Values

- Describe your ideal work environment.
- How do you prioritize tasks when you have multiple deadlines?
- How do you handle stress and pressure?
- Describe a time you had to manage multiple competing priorities.

## Teamwork

- Are you better working independently or in a team?
- Tell me about a time you went above and beyond for a team goal.
- How do you handle a team member who is not contributing?

## Initiative and Growth

- Tell me about a personal or professional project you started on your own.
- What's the most important thing you learned in your last project or internship?
- Describe a time you identified a problem before being asked to solve it.

## Tips for Strong Behavioral Answers

1. **Prepare 6–8 stories** that can be adapted to multiple question types
2. **Quantify outcomes** — "reduced build time by 30%", "mentored 3 junior developers"
3. **Show progression** — demonstrate that you learned from the experience
4. **Match to role** — choose stories relevant to the seniority and function being interviewed for
5. **Be concise** — a good STAR answer takes 90–120 seconds, not 5 minutes

## Fresher-Specific Guidance

If you have limited work experience:
- Use academic projects, open-source contributions, hackathons, internships
- Focus on YOUR individual contribution within a group project
- Transfer soft-skill examples from leadership roles in clubs/teams
- It is fine to say "In my project..." instead of "At my job..."
