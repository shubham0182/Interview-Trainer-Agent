# Behavioral Interview Questions — STAR Method

## What is the STAR Method?

The STAR method is the standard framework for answering behavioral interview questions:

- **S**ituation — Describe the context. Where were you? What was the challenge or opportunity?
- **T**ask — What was your specific responsibility or goal?
- **A**ction — What did YOU do? Use "I" not "we". Be specific about steps taken.
- **R**esult — What was the outcome? Quantify where possible.

## Common Behavioral Questions

### Teamwork and Collaboration
- Tell me about a time you worked on a difficult team project. What was your role?
- Describe a situation where you had a disagreement with a colleague. How did you resolve it?
- Give an example of a time you helped a teammate who was struggling.

### Leadership
- Tell me about a time you led a project or initiative, even without a formal leadership title.
- Describe a time when you had to influence others without direct authority.
- Give an example of when you had to make a difficult decision under pressure.

### Problem Solving
- Tell me about the most challenging technical problem you've solved. Walk me through your approach.
- Describe a time when you had to learn something new quickly to complete a task.
- Give an example of a time a project wasn't going well. What did you do?

### Communication
- Tell me about a time you had to explain a complex technical concept to a non-technical audience.
- Describe a situation where you gave or received critical feedback. What happened?
- Give an example of a time you had to manage stakeholder expectations.

### Adaptability
- Tell me about a time you had to adapt to a major change at work or in a project.
- Describe a situation where you had to work with incomplete information. How did you handle it?
- Give an example of a time you failed. What did you learn?

## Red Flags to Avoid

- Using "we" instead of "I" — interviewers want YOUR contribution
- No measurable result — always try to quantify the outcome
- Negative framing — avoid blaming others; focus on what you did
- Vague situations — pick specific, real examples with a clear beginning and end
- Generic answers — tailor to the company/role being interviewed for

## Sample Strong Answer

**Q: Tell me about a time you had to learn something new quickly.**

*STAR:*
**Situation**: During my final year project, our team decided mid-sprint to switch from a REST API to GraphQL to support our mobile client's flexible query needs.
**Task**: I was responsible for the backend API layer and had never worked with GraphQL before.
**Action**: I spent the first evening going through the official docs and a hands-on tutorial. The next day I built a small proof-of-concept resolver for one endpoint, got feedback from my team, then migrated all 12 endpoints over 3 days, writing tests alongside the migration.
**Result**: We delivered the feature on schedule. The mobile client team reported 40% fewer API round-trips, and I continued maintaining the GraphQL layer for the rest of the project.
